# Mycairos: The Kingdom of Bronze and Blood

## **Origins:**

---

The beginnings of **Mycairos** are more straightforward—at least, so it seems. Their records, written in **runes**, describe a people who claimed to descend from gods of war and fire. Their citadels were built atop hills and carved into cliffsides, their foundations designed to last through siege and storm. Yet something about Mycairos' origins does not add up. Fragments of myth suggest they were once wanderers—exiles from a shattered land to the far north. They arrived on the shores of Hellenoria, a harsh and unforgiving land, and bent it to their will. The myths speak of pacts with forgotten beings, though whether these were divine, infernal, or something worse is unclear.

The **Citadel of Mycarion**, the heart of the empire, housed not only kings but also priests and sorcerers. Inscribed tablets hint at rituals performed in secret chambers—rites to awaken something that should not have been disturbed. Many of these runes remain undeciphered, though the phrase "**the gift beneath stone**" appears repeatedly, fueling endless debates among scholars. Were they seeking treasure, power, or something darker? Some fear the truth is better left buried.

---

## **Peak:**

---

At the height of their power, the warriors of Mycairos were unparalleled. **Fortress cities** dotted the landscape, each ruled by a warrior-king whose loyalty was bought with fear and iron. Their conquests stretched across the continent, and they established an empire where tribute flowed in from conquered lands. Unlike the delicate enchantments of Nethrana, Mycairos favored **brutal magic**—wards of blood and stone, curses that withered crops, and weapons that could not be broken. Their scribes cataloged every victory with cold precision, and their heroes became legends—immortalized in song, if not in fact.

But something in these records feels... off. There are mentions of **strange disappearances** among the nobility, and statues of gods with features that seem subtly wrong. In the last decades before the fall, the Mycairos empire grew increasingly paranoid, building more fortresses than cities, as if they were not just at war with Nethrana—but with **something else** entirely.

---

## **Fall:**

---

The end of Mycairos is as sudden as it is baffling. One moment, they were on the verge of victory over Nethrana, their armies poised to take Velothis; the next, their cities lay abandoned, their halls echoing with silence. Fragments from their final years mention **storms that carried voices**, **shadows moving against the light**, and a growing madness that spread through the empire like wildfire. Some surviving texts suggest Mycairos unearthed something beneath the earth—an ancient, slumbering force—and that their doom was sealed the moment they disturbed it.

Whether their downfall came from within or through the curse of their enemies remains unknown. What is certain is that Mycairos’ once-mighty citadels stand as hollow shells. Those few who have dared enter their ruins report finding strange carvings—depictions of creatures and symbols **not recorded anywhere else**—and the sense of being watched. None stay long.
