# Nethrana: The Kingdom of Mirrors and Mist

## **Origins:**

---

No one knows the precise origin of **Nethrana**. Some ancient sources claim it emerged from the sea itself, its first settlers drawn from a vanished people who arrived on enchanted vessels. Others say Nethrana was a gift from an unknown god or an accident of powerful magic that reshaped the island of **Velothis**. Even the empire’s true name is debated: Nethrana might not have been what its people called it, but the only word left inscribed in glyphs that seem to reflect their essence.

The ruins suggest a people enamored with beauty and mystery, their walls lined with shimmering murals that seem to change when looked at too long. It’s said that the **Palace of Eronis** was so complex that even its architects were lost within its endless corridors of glass and enchanted stone. Some legends claim these halls held **portals**—gates that led not just across the empire but into strange dreamscapes, places where time and space blurred. Scholars have yet to decipher the meaning of Nethrana’s art and inscriptions; their language was pictorial, each symbol capable of shifting meaning depending on how the light touched it

---

## **Peak:**

---

Nethrana reached its height when trade vessels from Velothis ruled the seas. Inscriptions discovered on shipwrecks far from their shores tell of cargoes filled with enchanted textiles, illusions frozen in glass, and artifacts pulsing with dormant magic. Some scrolls suggest Nethrana’s wealth lay not in mere trade but in knowledge—hidden arcane secrets coveted by sorcerers and kings. **The Council of Eronis**—said to be composed of dream-walkers, seers, and illusionists—may have wielded influence over the minds of other rulers across the continent. Yet this is pure speculation; the Council's existence is recorded only in fragments, and the Nethranan texts that mention it are maddeningly incomplete.

---

## **Fall:**

---

The end of Nethrana is a void in history—an event shrouded in obscurity and marked only by sudden silence. In the last known inscription from Velothis, the words seem to twist into nonsense: "Beware the **Third Sun**, for what is seen shall not be saved." Some believe this was a prophecy of an impending disaster, though its meaning is hopelessly unclear.

What little we know suggests that **the Great War with Mycairos** triggered their decline, but the specific events remain unclear—were they overrun by force, betrayed from within, or undone by their own magic? There are hints of a **curse**, a subtle enchantment that turned the Nethranan dreamers into wanderers of their own minds, unable to distinguish illusion from reality. Their palaces were abandoned, their enchantments left to unravel. The last travelers who tried to explore the ruins of the Palace of Eronis reported encountering strange mirages—visions of impossible futures and endless hallways that seemed to shift with every step. None returned.
