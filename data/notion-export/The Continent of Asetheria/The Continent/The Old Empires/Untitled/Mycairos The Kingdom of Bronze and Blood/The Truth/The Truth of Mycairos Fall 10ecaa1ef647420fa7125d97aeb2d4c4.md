# The Truth of Mycairos Fall

Created: October 13, 2024 11:04 AM

The collapse of Mycairos was not due to enemy armies but to **something they unearthed beneath their own lands**—something that should have stayed buried. In the early years of the empire, Mycairos' priests discovered a network of ancient tunnels hidden beneath the cliffs of **Ekran**, filled with strange glyphs and artifacts. The Mycairan kings believed they had uncovered the remnants of a lost civilization, but the priests knew better. The tunnels were not remnants—they were **containment vaults**, holding a force older than gods.

Below the earth slumbered **the Nameless Hunger**, a primordial entity bound in stone and blood. Mycairos’ priests, tempted by the promise of power, sought to **awaken** this force, believing it could make the empire invincible. But the Nameless Hunger did not serve mortals. It fed on them. The rituals performed in secret chambers were not invocations—they were cracks in the seal, each spell weakening the ancient wards until the Hunger awoke.

The first signs were **subtle**: whispers in the night, soldiers who vanished without a trace, and strange symbols carved into stone by hands that should not have remembered them. But soon, the Hunger grew bold. Entire cities turned on themselves—**frenzies of bloodshed and madness** swept through the empire as the entity began to consume both mind and flesh. Warriors butchered their comrades, noble houses turned to dark rites, and statues of gods twisted into monstrous shapes overnight.

The rulers of Mycairos tried to fight back, building ever more **fortresses** to shield themselves from an enemy they could not see. But it was too late—the Nameless Hunger could not be fought, only delayed. In their desperation, the priests attempted one last ritual to **bind the Hunger** again, sacrificing the blood of thousands to reseal the ancient vaults. It worked—but only partially.

The Hunger vanished, but so did the souls of those involved in the ritual. Entire cities were left empty, as if the people within had been **erased** from existence. Those who survived the frenzy fled to distant lands, abandoning Mycairos to its cursed fate. Today, the citadels stand silent, their halls still marked by strange glyphs and carvings that scholars have yet to translate—**prayers, perhaps, or warnings**.

Some say that the Nameless Hunger is still awake, lurking in the shadows of those ruins, waiting for the day the seals will **crumble again**.
