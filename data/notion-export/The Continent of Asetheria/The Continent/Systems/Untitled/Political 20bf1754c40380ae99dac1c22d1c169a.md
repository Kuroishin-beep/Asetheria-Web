# Overview

Five years after the signing of the Treaty of Deiperdeum, the continent of Asetheria is a cauldron of simmering political tensions, a landscape scarred by 1,500 years of relentless warfare. The exhausted empires of the Imperium Invicta, Hellenoria, and Acheaoria may have laid down their swords, but the political machinations that fueled the millennia-long conflict have only just begun to adapt to the unsettling quiet of peace. The political state of each power is a complex and volatile mix of war-weariness, entrenched militarism, and the burgeoning seeds of internal strife.

## The Imperium Invicta

The Imperium Invicta, once a proud republic, has long since shed the trappings of representative rule in the crucible of perpetual war. The Senate still meets in the hallowed halls of its magnificent capital, but its function is now largely ceremonial, a rubber stamp for the will of the true power in the Imperium: the military.

### **The Dominus Militum**

For centuries, the Imperium has been led by a *Dominus Militum*, a "Master of the Soldiers," a supreme military commander who holds dictatorial power. This position, initially a temporary measure to deal with existential threats, has become a permanent institution. The current Dominus, the aging but still formidable General Valerius Maximus, is a veteran of the final decades of the war and was one of the key architects of the Treaty of Neutrality. His authority is absolute, backed by the loyalty of the legions he once led to countless victories.

### **The Council of Legates**

Beneath the Dominus Militum is the Council of Legates, comprised of the commanders of the Imperium's most powerful legions. This council is a viper's nest of ambition and rivalry. While they present a united front to the public, each Legate commands a personal army and views the others with suspicion. The five years of peace have only intensified their political maneuvering, as they are no longer distracted by external enemies. They are now carving out fiefdoms within the provinces they govern, building personal power bases in anticipation of the inevitable power vacuum that will be left by the aging Valerius.

### **The Demobilized Legions and the Rise of Warlords**

The greatest threat to the Imperium's stability comes from within. Millions of demobilized legionaries, men who have known nothing but war, have been promised land and pensions that the bankrupt state is struggling to provide. This has created a vast and volatile underclass of highly trained and disgruntled veterans. In the more remote provinces, some former officers have taken matters into their own hands, using their loyal troops to seize land and establish themselves as independent warlords, defying the authority of the Dominus Militum.

### **The Patrician Remnant**

The old patrician families, the descendants of the senators who once ruled the Republic, still hold immense wealth and social prestige. They resent the military's dominance and quietly plot a return to what they see as their rightful place as the rulers of the Imperium. They use their wealth to influence provincial governors and sow dissent among the populace, hoping to exploit any weakness in the military's grip on power.

## Hellenoria

Hellenoria, a league of maritime city-states, has weathered the long war by virtue of its decentralized nature. However, the very independence that ensured their survival is now a source of profound political instability. The five years of peace have seen the resurgence of old rivalries and the rise of new, dangerous ambitions.

### **The League of the Hellenes**

On paper, Hellenoria is governed by the League of the Hellenes, an assembly of representatives from each city-state who meet to decide on matters of common interest. In reality, the League is a forum for endless debate and political posturing, with each city-state jealously guarding its own sovereignty. The war forced a degree of cooperation, but with the immediate threat gone, the old divisions are re-emerging.

### **The Democratic Ideal vs. the Oligarchic Reality**

While many city-states, particularly the larger ones, cling to the ideals of democracy, the long war has empowered a wealthy elite of merchants, ship owners, and military commanders. In many cities, these oligarchs hold the true power, manipulating the democratic process to their own ends. This has created a growing class divide and a sense of disenfranchisement among the common citizens.

### **The Rise of the Tyrants**

The post-war chaos has created a fertile ground for the rise of tyrants. In several smaller city-states, charismatic and ruthless individuals, often former military commanders, have seized power, promising stability and order in exchange for absolute authority. These tyrants are a destabilizing force, often pursuing aggressive foreign policies to bolster their own legitimacy and enrich their followers. They pay lip service to the League but act with impunity, further weakening the confederacy.

### **The Naval Hegemony and its Discontents**

The most powerful city-state, a sprawling metropolis with the largest fleet, has traditionally held a position of naval hegemony within the League. During the war, this was accepted as a necessary evil. Now, in peacetime, the other city-states are beginning to resent its dominance, fearing that it will use its naval might to impose its will on them. This has led to the formation of smaller, rival alliances within the League, creating a complex web of shifting allegiances and potential for internal conflict.

## Acheaoria

The Acheaoria, an empire of immense size and fabulous wealth, is a political entity defined by its rigid hierarchy and the absolute power of its "King of Kings." However, beneath the glittering facade of opulence and ceremony, the empire is rotting from within, its foundations weakened by centuries of corruption, court intrigue, and the simmering resentment of its diverse and often oppressed peoples.

### **The King of Kings and the Satrapy System**

The empire is ruled by the King of Kings, a semi-divine figure who resides in a magnificent palace at the heart of the empire. His authority is absolute, but his ability to project that authority across his vast domain is limited. The empire is divided into numerous satrapies, or provinces, each governed by a satrap appointed by the king. These satraps are virtual monarchs in their own right, and their loyalty to the central government is often a matter of convenience.

### **The Court of Vipers**

The royal court is a hotbed of intrigue and conspiracy. The various noble families, each with their own ambitions, vie for the king's favor and plot against their rivals. The eunuchs who manage the royal household wield immense influence, and the succession to the throne is often a bloody and chaotic affair. The five years of peace have given these court factions more time and resources to devote to their power struggles, creating an atmosphere of paranoia and instability at the very center of the empire.

### **The Restless Satrapies**

Many of the satrapies are inhabited by peoples who were conquered by the Acheaoriacenturies ago and who have never fully accepted their rule. During the long war, the empire was able to suppress dissent through a combination of brute force and the promise of protection from the other great powers. Now that the external threat has receded, old grievances are resurfacing. Nationalist movements are gaining traction in several key provinces, and some satraps are secretly fanning the flames of rebellion, hoping to break away from the empire and establish their own independent kingdoms.

### **The Magi and the Faith**

The powerful priestly caste of the Magi holds significant sway over the religious and cultural life of the empire. They are the guardians of the state religion and often act as a check on the power of the King of Kings. The Magi are a conservative force, resistant to change and deeply suspicious of foreign influences. They are a powerful unifying force within the core of the empire but are often at odds with the diverse religious beliefs of the conquered peoples, further fueling ethnic and religious tensions.
