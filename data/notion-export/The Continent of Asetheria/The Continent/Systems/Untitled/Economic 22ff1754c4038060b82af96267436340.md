# Imperium Invicta

## Overview

The Imperium Invicta, a sprawling, disciplined empire modeled on the legions and has an economy built for total war. For centuries, its vast resources were funneled into the singular purpose of military conquest and defense. Now, in the sudden stillness of peace, the gears of this immense war machine are grinding to a halt, revealing both its immense strength and its critical vulnerabilities.

## Agriculture

The heartland of the Imperium is a patchwork of vast latifundia, massive agricultural estates worked by a populace tied to the land through a system of serfdom and indentured servitude. The war led to the consolidation of land into the hands of powerful families and in the heart of it is the city Ephelanum. For centuries, the primary agricultural output was grain, destined to feed the insatiable appetite of the legions. The five years of peace have seen a slow and tentative shift. Some landowners are experimenting with more lucrative cash crops like olives and grapes for wine production, but the infrastructure to transport and trade these goods on a large scale is still in its infancy. The immense demand for grain has subsided, leading to a surplus in some regions and a subsequent crash in prices, causing unrest among the rural populace.

## Trade and Commerce

The legendary paved roads of the Imperium, once arteries of military movement, are now cautiously being repurposed for commerce. However, centuries of conflict have made trade a risky venture. Banditry is rampant, often perpetrated by demobilized soldiers with no other skills than violence. A complex system of internal tariffs and taxes, designed to fund the war effort, still stifles inter-provincial trade. The port cities, once bustling hubs of maritime trade, are a shadow of their former selves, their fleets decimated by centuries of naval warfare with Hellenoria.

## Resource and Industry

The Imperium's industrial might is centered around its mining operations and its state-run armories and forges. The mountains of the southern provinces are rich in iron, lead, and tin, and other magical ores, the essential ingredients for the legions' weaponry and armor. These industries, however, were entirely state-controlled, and the sudden drop in military demand has led to mass unemployment in these regions. The transition to producing civilian goods is slow and inefficient. The once-unwavering demand for iron has plummeted, causing economic hardship in the mining communities.

## Infrastructure

The Imperium's road network is its greatest asset, a testament to its engineering prowess. However, after 1500 years of constant use and neglect in less critical regions, many of these roads are in a state of disrepair. Bridges have collapsed, and waystations have fallen into ruin. The new era of peace has brought a renewed focus on infrastructure repair, with the state offering contracts to private companies, a new and somewhat chaotic development in the rigidly controlled Imperial economy.

## Wealth and Labor

Wealth in the Imperium is heavily concentrated in the hands of the patrician class and the highest echelons of the military. A vast gulf exists between the opulent few and the impoverished masses. The five years of peace have exacerbated this divide. Demobilized soldiers, promised land and pensions that the drained state coffers can barely afford, are a volatile and disaffected segment of the population. The traditional reliance on a large, disciplined workforce is now a source of social tension, as millions of former soldiers and workers in war-related industries find themselves without purpose or income.

# Hellenoria

## Overview

Hellenoria, a confederation of fiercely independent city-states united by a common culture and a reliance on the sea, has endured the long war through naval prowess and a decentralized, adaptable economy. The sea is their lifeblood, and the five years of peace have been a desperate scramble to rebuild their shattered fleets and re-establish their dominance over the waves.

## Agriculture

The rugged, mountainous terrain of Hellenoria is ill-suited for large-scale agriculture. The city-states have always relied on maritime trade to supplement their own production of olives, grapes, and citrus fruits. During the war, this dependence was a critical vulnerability, as the Imperium's land-based power and Acheaoria’s vast resources could choke their supply lines. The five years of peace have seen a renewed urgency in maximizing their limited arable land, with innovative terracing and irrigation projects being undertaken. However, they remain heavily reliant on food imports.

## Trade and Commerce

Trade is the very soul of Hellenoria. Their merchants are renowned for their cunning and their willingness to venture into dangerous waters. The war devastated their mercantile fleet, and the past five years have been a period of frantic shipbuilding. The city-states are once again sending out feelers, re-establishing contact with old trading partners and seeking new markets. They have a sophisticated understanding of finance, with nascent banking houses and a complex system of loans and insurance for maritime ventures. However, piracy, a constant menace, has only been emboldened by the post-war chaos.

## Resource and Industry

Hellenoria lacks the vast natural resources of the Imperium. Their industry is focused on skilled craftsmanship: pottery, metalwork, and, above all, shipbuilding. The demand for their high-quality, fast triremes and trading vessels is a key driver of their economic recovery. The secrets of "Hellenic fire," a potent naval weapon, are a closely guarded state secret and a significant deterrent against potential aggressors.

## Infrastructure

The infrastructure of Hellenoria is maritime. Their cities are built around well-defended harbors, and their "roads" are the sea lanes. The war saw the destruction of many of their port facilities, and rebuilding them is a top priority. Lighthouses are being repaired, and harbor defenses are being strengthened, a clear sign that the peace is viewed as a temporary respite.

## Wealth and Labor

Wealth in Hellenoria is more fluid than in the Imperium, concentrated in the hands of successful merchants, ship owners, and the political elite of the city-states. While a significant portion of the population are free citizens, there is also a large class of non-citizen residents (*metics*) who form the backbone of the artisan and laboring classes. The war took a heavy toll on their manpower, and there is a shortage of skilled sailors and craftsmen. This has led to a rise in wages for these professions, but also a greater reliance on foreign labor.

# Acheaoria

## Overview

The Acheaoria, a vast and ancient empire with a history stretching back millennia before the long war, is a land of immense wealth and staggering inequality. Their economy is based on centralized control, a sophisticated bureaucracy, and the exploitation of vast natural resources and conquered peoples.

## Agriculture

Acheaoria is blessed with fertile river valleys that produce a massive surplus of grain, fruits, and exotic spices. Their agriculture is highly organized, with a complex system of irrigation canals and state-controlled granaries. The "King of Kings" is theoretically the owner of all land, which is administered by a powerful class of satraps (provincial governors). During the war, this agricultural abundance was a key advantage, allowing them to sustain their vast armies. In peacetime, this surplus is a source of immense wealth, traded for luxury goods from across the continent.

## Trade and Commerce

The Acheaoria control the overland trade routes to the east, the fabled "Silk Road" of Asetheria. Their caravans, laden with silks, spices, and precious metals, are a target for raiders, but the empire maintains a network of fortified caravanserais and a dedicated corps of guards to protect this vital economic artery. The Imperial bureaucracy, however, imposes heavy taxes and tolls on this trade, enriching the state but also creating resentment among the merchant class. The port cities on their southern coast are also beginning to see a revival in maritime trade, though they are wary of the resurgent Hellenorian merchants.

## Resource and Industry

Acheaoria is rich in gold, silver, and precious stones, which are mined extensively and used to fund the lavish lifestyle of the court and the bureaucracy. Their artisans are masters of intricate metalwork, carpet weaving, and the production of luxurious textiles. These high-value goods are a major export. However, much of the heavy industry, such as iron and bronze production for their armies, was reliant on the labor of subject peoples, and the end of the war has not brought an end to their exploitation.

## Infrastructure

The "Royal Roads" of Acheaoria are a marvel of engineering, connecting the vast empire and facilitating the rapid movement of armies, messengers, and trade caravans. Like the Imperium, the maintenance of this extensive network is a constant challenge, especially in the more rebellious and far-flung satrapies.

## Wealth and Labor

The distribution of wealth in Acheaoria is grotesquely uneven. The royal family and the high-ranking nobility live in unimaginable luxury, while the majority of the population, including a vast number of slaves and subjugated peoples, live in poverty. The bureaucracy is a bloated and often corrupt entity, with officials at every level enriching themselves at the expense of the populace. The five years of peace have seen a rise in internal tensions, as the demobilized soldiers, many of whom are from conquered lands, return to a life of hardship and discrimination. The simmering resentment of the oppressed peoples is a tinderbox waiting for a spark.
