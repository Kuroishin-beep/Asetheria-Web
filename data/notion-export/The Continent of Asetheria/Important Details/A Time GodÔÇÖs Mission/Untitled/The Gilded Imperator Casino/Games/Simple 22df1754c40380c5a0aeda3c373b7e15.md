### 1. Siege Breaker

A fast-paced dice game of pure chance, named in honor of the Citadel's famous defensive victories. It’s popular with soldiers and common folk alike for its simplicity and speed.

**How to Play:**

The game is played with three six-sided dice (3d6). The goal is to "break the siege" by rolling higher than the "Wall," which is a fixed number set by the house at **11**.

1. A player places their bet.
2. The player rolls 3d6.
3. If the total of the dice is **12 or higher**, the player wins and doubles their bet.
4. If the total is **11 or lower**, they lose their bet to the house.
5. Rolling a **triple** (e.g., three 4s) is a critical success! The player triples their bet instead of doubling it. Rolling three 1s is a critical failure, and the player loses double their bet to the house.

**Mechanics & Skills:**

- **Simplicity is key:** This game is designed to be almost entirely luck-based, making it accessible to everyone.
- **Sleight of Hand (Dexterity):** A player could attempt to use weighted dice. This would be a DC 15 Sleight of Hand check against the dealer's passive Perception. If caught, the consequences are swift and severe, likely involving the formidable casino guards.

**Rewards:**

The standard reward is a simple doubling of the wager. The thrill comes from its fast pace, allowing players to win or lose money quickly. A house "bounty" might be offered for anyone who can roll three 6s ("The Catapult Shot"), offering a bonus payout of 10 times the original bet.

### 2. Caesar's Coin

A simple game of prediction and nerve, played with a single, ornate coin minted with the profile of the current Caertheian Duke on one side (Heads) and the Citadel's crest on the other (Tails).

**How to Play:**

1. Up to four players can play against the dealer. All players place their bets.
2. The dealer takes a single, heavy coin and places it under a cup without looking at it.
3. The dealer asks, "Head of the Duke, or Shield of the Citadel?"
4. Each player calls their choice.
5. The dealer lifts the cup, revealing the coin's face.
6. Players who guessed correctly win back their bet plus an equal amount (a 1-to-1 payout). Players who guessed incorrectly lose their bet.

**Mechanics & Skills:**

- **Insight (Wisdom):** A player might try to read the dealer for any subtle "tells." This isn't about seeing the coin, but rather about spotting a pattern in the dealer's behavior or their method of flipping and catching the coin. A successful DC 13 Insight check might grant the player a feeling that the dealer is trying to subtly influence the outcome, giving them an edge on their next guess.
- **Perception (Wisdom):** A character might try to listen to the sound the coin makes as it lands or notice a slight imperfection on the table that might favor one outcome. A high DC (17 or 18) could reveal such a minute detail.

**Rewards:**

A simple 1-to-1 payout. This is a low-stakes, social game. Winning five times in a row might earn a player a complimentary drink from the dealer or a small, non-magical token of the casino's esteem.

### 3. Desert Bloom

A card game for 2-5 players that uses a standard 52-card deck. The name is a poetic reference to the idea of finding fortune in a place once besieged by desert foes.

**How to Play:**

The goal is to get a hand as close to **13** as possible without going over. Aces are worth 1, face cards (Jack, Queen, King) are worth 10, and all other cards are worth their face value.

1. Every player places an ante into the pot.
2. The dealer gives each player two cards, face down.
3. Players look at their cards. They can choose to "Stand" with their current total or "Draw" one more card to try and get closer to 13.
4. After everyone has made their choice, all players reveal their hands.
5. The player with the hand closest to 13 without exceeding it wins the entire pot.
6. If a player's hand totals over 13, they "Wither" and are out of the round.
7. A perfect hand of 13 is called a "Bloom." A player with a Bloom wins the pot immediately, and all other players must pay them a bonus amount equal to their original ante.
8. In case of a tie, the tied players split the pot.

**Mechanics & Skills:**

- **Deception (Charisma):** A player with a poor hand might try to bluff, acting confident to intimidate other players into standing on a lower number, fearing the bluffer might have a perfect 13. This can be contested by other players' Insight.
- **Card-Counting (Intelligence):** While not requiring a specific skill check for this simple version, a character with a high Intelligence could be described by the DM as having an easier time tracking which cards have already been played, giving them a slight advantage in deciding whether to draw or stand.

**Rewards:**

The winner takes the pot of antes. The "Bloom" provides a significant bonus, making it the most sought-after outcome. This game encourages more player interaction than the other two, making it a good choice for a more roleplay-focused group.
