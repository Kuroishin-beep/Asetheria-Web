### 1. Dragon's Hoard

A high-stakes game of press-your-luck and shrewd betting, masquerading as a simple dice game. The goal is to amass the most valuable "hoard" of gems without getting burned by the dragon.

**How to Play:**

The game is played with a set of 20 custom dice, each with faces representing different gems (Diamond, Ruby, Emerald, Sapphire), a Gold Coin, and a Dragon's Head. Each gem has a point value: Diamond (10), Ruby (8), Emerald (6), Sapphire (4), and Gold Coin (2). The Dragon's Head is a bust.

A round begins with each player placing an ante. Then, the first player starts their turn by rolling three dice. They can choose to "bank" their current total and end their turn, adding the points to their score, or they can "press their luck" and roll an additional die. They can continue to roll one die at a time, accumulating points.

However, if at any point a Dragon's Head is rolled, the player's turn is over, they lose all points accumulated *that round*, and they must pay a penalty to the pot equal to their original ante.

After each player has had a turn, the player with the highest banked score wins the pot.

**Mechanics & Skills:**

- **Sleight of Hand (Dexterity):** A player can attempt to subtly swap out a die before rolling. This is a contested check against the dealer's and other players' passive Perception. If successful, they can replace one of the house dice with one of their own (perhaps weighted or marked). Failure results in being caught, likely leading to ejection from the game or worse.
- **Insight (Wisdom):** A player can try to read their opponents to see if they are bluffing about their confidence in their rolls or trying to goad others into pressing their luck.
- **Gambling Set Proficiency:** A character proficient with a gambling set can add their proficiency bonus to a single roll of their choice once per game.

**Rewards:**

The winner takes the entire pot of antes and any penalties paid. For higher-stakes games, the casino might offer a "Dragon's Prize," a non-monetary reward like a piece of valuable information, a map to a hidden treasure, or a favor from a powerful individual, to the player who achieves the highest single-round score of the night.

### 2. Arcane Scramble

A fast-paced card game that blends magical knowledge with quick thinking and a bit of luck. The game uses a special deck of cards, each depicting a different spell component.

**How to Play:**

The game is played with a deck of 52 "Arcane Scramble" cards. Each card features a spell component (e.g., a pinch of bat guano, a sprig of nightshade, a cat's eye agate). The goal is to be the first to form a valid "spell" by collecting the correct components.

At the start of the game, a "Spell Formula" card is drawn from a separate deck. This card lists three to five components needed for a specific spell (the DM can create these or use actual spell components from the Player's Handbook). For example, the "Fire Bolt" formula might require "Sulfur," "Bat Guano," and a "Red Dragon Scale."

Each player is dealt a hand of five cards. On their turn, a player can either draw a card from the deck or trade a card with an opponent (who cannot refuse the trade but gets to choose which of their cards to give away). The first player to collect all the necessary components for the displayed Spell Formula shouts "Incant!" and wins the round.

**Mechanics & Skills:**

- **Arcana (Intelligence):** Before the game begins, a player can make an Arcana check. A successful check allows them to recall the components for one of the Spell Formulas in the deck, giving them an advantage when it appears.
- **Deception (Charisma):** When trading, a player can use Deception to try and convince an opponent to give them a specific card they need, perhaps by feigning disinterest or suggesting another card is more valuable.
- **Sleight of Hand (Dexterity):** A player can attempt to palm a card from the deck or swap a card from their hand with one on the table. This is a contested check against the dealer's and other players' passive Perception.

**Rewards:**

The winner of each round receives a payout from the pot, which is funded by an initial buy-in from each player. Winning multiple rounds in a row might attract the attention of a wizard or artificer in the casino, who could offer the player a job or a rare magical item in exchange for their "quick and knowledgeable hands."

### 3. Owlbear's Rage

A game of pure chance and thrilling spectacle, centered around the unpredictable fury of a caged owlbear. This game is often found in less-reputable gambling dens or underground fighting pits.

**How to Play:**

A large, reinforced cage contains an owlbear. The floor of the cage is marked with a grid of numbered squares. Players place their bets on which square the owlbear will attack at the end of a "Rage Round."

A Rage Round begins with the handler goading the owlbear, often by poking it with a long pole or using loud noises. The Dungeon Master then rolls 2d10 to determine the coordinates of the square the owlbear attacks. For example, a roll of 3 and 8 would mean the owlbear strikes square (3,8).

Players can bet on a single number, a row, a column, or a quadrant of the grid. The payouts are higher for more specific bets.

**Mechanics & Skills:**

- **Animal Handling (Wisdom):** A player might attempt to subtly influence the owlbear's direction of attack through calming or agitating gestures. This would be a difficult check (DC 18 or higher) and, if successful, might allow the player to reroll one of the d10s. Failure could result in the owlbear becoming enraged at the character, focusing its next attack on the square closest to them.
- **Perception (Wisdom):** A character might notice a subtle bias in the cage's construction or the handler's technique that makes certain squares more likely to be attacked. A successful check could give the player advantage on their next bet.

**Rewards:**

Payouts are determined by the rarity of the bet. A single-number bet might pay out 35 to 1, while a bet on a quadrant might pay 3 to 1. A "Jackpot" could be offered for correctly predicting the exact square three times in a row, potentially winning a significant prize like a trained guard drake or a deed to a small property.

### 4. Mindflayer's Gambit

A tense and cerebral card game of psionic prowess and mental fortitude, often played in exclusive, high-society casinos or among the magically inclined.

**How to Play:**

This is a two-player game played with a standard deck of cards. The goal is to have a higher card than your opponent in a series of three "Mental Duels."

Each player is dealt three cards, face down. The players then engage in a round of betting. After the betting, both players simultaneously reveal their first card. The player with the higher card wins the first "duel." This process is repeated for the second and third cards. The player who wins two out of the three duels wins the entire pot.

The twist is the "Psionic Interference" phase. Before each reveal, a player can attempt to use their own mental abilities to influence the outcome.

**Mechanics & Skills:**

- **Insight (Wisdom) vs. Deception (Charisma):** This is the core of the game. Players are constantly trying to read their opponent's intentions and bluff about the strength of their own hand. This is resolved through a series of opposed checks. A successful Insight check might reveal if the opponent is bluffing, while a successful Deception check can mislead the opponent into betting high on a weak hand.
- **Telepathy or other Psychic Abilities:** If a character has telepathy, they can attempt to subtly probe their opponent's mind for a glimpse of their cards. This would require a contested Intelligence (Arcana) or Wisdom (Insight) check against the opponent's saving throw. If detected, the cheater faces severe consequences.
- **Spells like *Detect Thoughts* or *Suggestion*:** Using such magic would be considered blatant cheating and would likely result in immediate and forceful removal from the establishment.

**Rewards:**

Mindflayer's Gambit is typically played for very high stakes, with large sums of gold or even magical items on the line. The true reward, however, is the reputation gained from being a skilled player. A renowned Mindflayer's Gambit champion might be approached by spies or nobles for their ability to read people and maintain a calm demeanor under pressure.

### 5. Golem's Gauntlet

A unique and destructive game of chance that combines elements of a demolition derby with magical constructs. This game is a major spectacle, often the centerpiece of a casino's entertainment.

**How to Play:**

Two or more small, magically animated golems are placed in an arena filled with obstacles and traps. Each golem is assigned a color. Players bet on which golem will be the last one standing.

Before the "Gauntlet" begins, the golems are programmed with a simple set of instructions (e.g., "attack the nearest golem," "move towards the center," "avoid traps"). The DM secretly rolls a d6 for each golem to determine its primary directive for the round from a pre-set table. The golems then act on their initiative count, following their programming.

The arena can contain various hazards, such as pits, crushing walls, or areas of magical energy that can damage or disable the golems.

**Mechanics & Skills:**

- **Tinker's Tools or Arcana (Intelligence):** A character with proficiency in Tinker's Tools or a high Arcana skill might be able to spot a flaw in one of the golem's construction or programming before the match begins. A successful check could grant them insight into a golem's likely behavior, giving them an edge in betting.
- **Investigation (Intelligence):** A player can survey the arena before the match, making an Investigation check to identify the most dangerous traps and predict how they might affect the golems' movements.
- **Cheating:** A magically-inclined character might try to cast a spell like *Grease* in front of an opponent's golem or use *Mage Hand* to subtly trigger a trap. This would be risky and require a successful Sleight of Hand or Stealth check to avoid detection by the arena's overseers.

**Rewards:**

The payouts are based on the odds assigned to each golem, which are determined by its past performance and perceived strengths. A longshot victor could result in a massive windfall. The grand prize for a major Golem's Gauntlet tournament could be one of the deactivated golems itself, a valuable and powerful prize for any adventuring party.
