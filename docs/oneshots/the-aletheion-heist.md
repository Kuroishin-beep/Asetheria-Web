# The Aletheion Heist

*A Hellenorian one-shot for **five 4th-level characters**. Roughly 4 hours: one social act, one casing act, one heist act. Natural break point after Act II.*

> **The pitch:** An occult society has already bought your crew's way into the most guarded building in the capital. All you have to do is pick up what they paid for, walk into the empire's museum of official history, and steal the one exhibit the Arcane Academy sealed behind four rings of mirrored glass — on the only night it will ever be unguarded enough to try.

**Where it sits in canon:** [[Hellarchon City]], capital of [[Hellenoria]] — *Helarchon* in the old spelling, *Aetherion* to the natives. The patron is [[The Eclipsed Mirror]], the occult magical society dedicated to [[Hecate, The Mistress of the Arcane]]. The black market runs through [[The Obsidian Masquerade]]. The rival crew is [[Tenebral Covenant]]. The museum's collection is the history of the city itself — including what was dug out of the ruins of [[Mycairos: The Kingdom of Bronze and Blood]].

---

## The Reward (DM summary, up front)

A fair market rate for a five-person crew on a job like this is **1,000 gp** (200 gp a head). Because the target sits under an Academy seal — and because the Truth-Flame is *in the building* that night — the Eclipsed Mirror pays a hazard premium of half again:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Contract total** | **1,500 gp** (300 gp each) |
| Advance | 600 gp — mixed coin plus a pouch of uncut amethysts |
| On delivery | 900 gp in coin — **or** swap the 900 gp for two divinity-infused [[Drachmae]] stamped with Hecate's triple face (500 gp market each; up to 1,000 gp each at a temple of Hecate, "but negotiable" — and spending them tells Hecate's faithful exactly who you are) |
| Sweetener | If the **Lysikleis** (see below) still holds its last charge after the job, the crew keeps it |

Loot lifted *inside* the museum is theirs too — see **Exhibits & Fence Values**. Every famous piece they take raises the heat in the aftermath.

## Background — What Is Actually True

Long before the empire, the warrior-kings of [[Mycairos: The Kingdom of Bronze and Blood]] landed on these shores and bent them to their will. Their runes repeat one phrase scholars still can't close the book on: **"the gift beneath stone."** Then their cities emptied in a single generation — storms that carried voices, shadows moving against the light — and nobody knows why.

Three years ago, an Academy dig under **Thalos Ridge** — the same ridge where archaeologists keep finding mass graves the official founding story says cannot exist — broke into a Mycairan under-citadel. Among the bronze they brought up was a key: forearm-long, bronze wound around bone, warm to the touch, with something like a heartbeat in it. Two conservators who handled it began sleepwalking toward the ridge. The Arcane Academy sealed it inside the **Seal of the Fourth Mirror** and parked it in the one place nobody robs: the state museum.

[[The Eclipsed Mirror]] believes the key — the **Kleis Mycarion** — opens the gift beneath stone, and that the gift is a crossroads: a door of the kind their mistress keeps. They cannot move openly; the Academy knows their faces. So they bought a seal-breaker from the black market, paid in full, and now they need five deniable professionals.

Tomorrow the museum unveils its **Treaty of Myrianth anniversary exhibition**. The morning after, the Kleis moves to the Academy's deep-vaults forever. Tonight is the only night.

## Strong Start

Read or paraphrase:

> Dusk in Aetherion is violet — not sunset-violet; something older that the locals have stopped asking about. You're in the back room of a harbor taverna where the dice game is Siege Breaker and the house always wins, when the widow at the corner table lifts her veil just enough for you to see she isn't crying. She slides a pouch across the table. It's heavy. "Six hundred now. Nine hundred when the museum's star exhibit is in my hand. Everything the job needs has already been bought — you'll simply be *picking it up*. My circle asks only three things: don't kill anyone, don't be seen taking it, and don't — under any circumstances — touch the key with your skin."

She names herself **Madame Trioditis**. She will answer exactly three questions honestly (track them; she counts). Then she tells them where the seal-breaker waits: *the Night Agora, beneath the Old Harbor, tonight. Wear masks. It's already paid for — ask the Locksmith's Chapel for the Lysikleis.*

## Act I — The Night Agora

**The black market.** Once a moon, [[The Obsidian Masquerade]] — the secret society sworn to [[Dionysus, The God of Festivity]] — throws a masked revel in a sea-cave warehouse under the Old Harbor, and the revel *is* the market. Wine, drums, lantern-light on black water, and between the dancers: stalls. The [[Tenebral Covenant]] fences here. So does worse.

**Getting in.** The door-warden (a satyr in an obsidian goat-mask) requires two things: a mask, and **"a truth for a mask"** — each guest must speak one true secret aloud at the threshold. The warden knows a lie when he hears one (treat as passive Insight 17; a DC 15 Deception sells a *trivial* truth as a deep one). A refused guest can instead pay 25 gp or win a round of Dragon's Hoard against the warden. This is a great 10-minute spotlight for every player: what does each PC confess?

**The pickup.** The **Locksmith's Chapel** is a stall built inside a beached ship's hull, hung with ten thousand keys. The fence, **Old Kyros** of the Tenebral Covenant, honors the purchase without argument — the Mirror's credit is good — and produces:

> **The Lysikleis** *("the Loosening Key")* — wondrous item, no attunement. A handleless key of Virellarion **memorysteel** ([[The Virellarion Family - Count]] work; the metal remembers its last shape). Pressed against a lock, glyph, or seal for 1 minute, it *learns* the shape. Expend 1 charge to open that lock or suppress that seal/glyph for 1 hour. **2 charges remain** (the Count's demonstrator spent the first). It hums, very slightly, like it's thinking.

**The complication.** As Kyros hands it over, a broker in a grey moth-mask — **Phalaina**, an agent of [[The Nightweaver Syndicate]] — glides in and claims *she* has a prior purchase order, and offers the crew **double the Mirror's whole fee (3,000 gp)** to walk the Lysikleis to her patrons instead. She is lying about the purchase order (DC 13 Insight) but not about the money. If refused, she smiles, toasts them — and tries to lift the Lysikleis off whoever carries it before they leave the cave (opposed Sleight of Hand vs. passive Perception; she has +7). If caught, she dissolves into the dancers. **Do not let this become a bloodbath** — the Masquerade's satyr-wardens (use twice the party's number, they won't be fought here) enforce the peace of the revel. Phalaina reappears in the aftermath if the crew ever wants a second buyer.

**What Kyros will sell besides** (if asked): museum gossip (he fenced for a laundress there — see *Secrets & Clues* 4 and 5), two doses of drowsy wine (DC 13 Con or unconscious 1 hour, 50 gp each), a stolen conservator's badge (30 gp, advantage on one Deception inside the museum).

## Act II — Casing the Aletheion

### The museum

**The Aletheion** — from the Greek *alētheia*, "truth; literally *un-forgetting*" — the Imperial Mouseion of Helarchon, on Museum Hill in the Scholars' District, in the violet shadow of the Arcane Academy. The name is the empire's little joke on itself, though not the way it intends: every label in the building has been through the censors, and the wags of the city call it **the Lētheion** — the House of Forgetting. Both names are truer than the curators would like.

It is the history of the city told in five rooms — the approved version. [[Selenarion of Helarchon]] wrote the labels' first drafts; the Truth-Flame rewrote them.

**Tonight's situation (the one-night window):** the museum is *closed for re-labeling* on the eve of the anniversary exhibition. Inside overnight: a skeleton watch (Warden Dexios + 4 phylakes instead of the usual twelve), the curator working late, and — the wrinkle nobody expects — **Inquisitor Callixta Vireon of the Truth-Flame** with two templars, auditing the Sealed Annex's labels until dawn. At dawn, Academy custodians arrive to crate the Kleis for the deep-vaults.

### Casing it by daylight

The museum is open to the public until dusk (2 sp entry). A daytime visit lets the crew see every room on the map except the Bronze Vault stair (roped off, "conservation"), watch patrol habits, count guards, and clock the exits. Reward good questions:

- DC 12 **Perception** — the four bronze hoplite statues in the rotunda stand in *different poses* than this morning.
- DC 13 **Investigation** — display cases carry a hair-thin silver thread in the seams: alarm-ward (opening a case without the warden's rod rings every bell in the building; +2 Heat).
- DC 14 **History/Arcana** — the Seal of the Fourth Mirror downstairs isn't just a lock, it's a *tattletale*: reach through it and it marks you for Academy scrying (this is why the Mirror said *don't touch the key*; the Lysikleis exists to prevent exactly this).
- Talking to staff: **Warden Dexios** (night warden) drinks at the Siege Breaker taverna and owes the Tenebral Covenant 90 gp of dice debt. He can be bought (100 gp clears his debt: he'll "forget" to relight the undercroft lanterns and misplace the case-rod for one hour), befriended, or blackmailed. He will *not* let anyone get hurt — push him and he panics.

### Three ways in

- **A. The Label Night (front door, social).** Conservators, caterers for the dawn unveiling, and Truth-Flame clerks come and go by the service dock all night. Forged or stolen credentials (Kyros's badge; DC 13 Deception at the dock, DC 15 if the Inquisitor's templar is doing the checking) get the crew inside *legitimately* — but inside on a leash, escorted between rooms until they slip away (group Stealth DC 12).
- **B. The Rain-Cistern (underneath, wet).** Museum Hill sits on an old aqueduct cistern. A grate in the cistern wall (rusted shut; DC 15 Athletics or DC 15 thieves' tools, quietly — 17 loudly, +1 Heat) opens into the flooded undercroft passage: 60 ft of chest-deep black water (swim/wade, DC 12 Athletics group check; failure means dropped gear or noise), past a **drowned ward-glyph** (DC 14 Perception to spot the shimmer under the water; DC 14 Arcana to unpick, or the Lysikleis learns-and-suppresses it *without spending a charge* — it's a minor glyph; triggering it = +2 Heat and 3d6 thunder, DC 13 Con for half). Emerges in Undercroft storage, 40 ft from the Vault stair.
- **C. The Skylight Walk (above, high).** The Academy viaduct passes one alley from the museum roof (DC 13 Athletics jump-and-catch, 30 ft drop if failed). The rotunda skylight's frame opens with tools (DC 13) and 60 ft of rope reaches the rotunda floor — directly between the four bronze hoplites. Silent if done well (group Stealth DC 13); the drop bypasses every door in the building.

### The Lantern Clock (heat)

Track heat 0–6. +1 whenever the crew is loud, leaves a body-shaped absence, or fails a listed check that says so.

| Heat | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Dexios does an extra round; lanterns relit in dark rooms |
| 2 | Phylakes pair up; one watches the rotunda balcony |
| 3 | **The four bronze hoplites step off their plinths** and begin patrolling (they know the building; they don't sleep) |
| 4 | Inquisitor Vireon emerges from the Annex with her templars, casts *light* on the rotunda, and starts *interviewing* anyone found — she wants arrests, not corpses |
| 5 | A runner is sent; 6 city phylakes arrive at the front portico in 10 minutes |
| 6 | Full lockdown: doors barred (DC 17 to force), the dawn Academy custodians arrive early. Time to improvise a legend. |

## Act III — The Heist

### The Bronze Vault

Down the roped-off stair, behind a bronze-faced door (Dexios's key, the case-rod trick, or DC 15 thieves' tools). The room is Mycairan bronze floor to ceiling — the museum built its vault out of the excavation itself. Pressure plates tile the floor's center aisle (DC 14 Perception to spot the seams, DC 12 Acrobatics to move along the wall-lip; a plate rings a deep bell — +2 Heat).

At the center, on a basalt plinth: **four counter-rotating rings of mirrored glass**, nested like an armillary sphere, and inside them, suspended in nothing, **the Kleis Mycarion**. In every mirror-ring the reflected room is *slightly wrong* — in one of them, something in the reflection is standing next to you (it isn't; probably).

**The Seal of the Fourth Mirror:**

- **The intended way:** press the Lysikleis to the outermost ring for 1 minute (it learns), expend 1 charge — the rings slow, unwind, and hang dead in the air over 3 quiet rounds. No alarm. No mark. The Mirror's plan works exactly as advertised.
- **Reaching through anyway:** 3d8 psychic (DC 14 Int save for half) and the toucher is **mirror-marked** — an Academy scry-beacon for the next 7 days (this is a *delayed* catastrophe, not an alarm; let them win tonight and be hunted tomorrow).
- **Brute ritual:** DC 20 Arcana over 10 minutes (three failures = backlash as above).
- **Smashing the rings:** works instantly. Every bell in the building rings (+3 Heat) — and see below, immediately.

### The Twist — the bronze remembers

The moment the Seal is down and the Kleis leaves its plinth *by any method*, every Mycairan bronze in the building **remembers what it was for.** Verdigris tears run. The Weeping Bronze — the colossus head in the vault's corner niche — grinds upward on a body of assembled exhibit-pieces: the **Bronze Revenant of Mycarion**. The four hoplites (wherever they are on the Heat track) turn and begin walking toward the vault.

**This is an escape encounter, not a boss-kill.** The Revenant is slow (25 ft), cannot leave the museum grounds (it is bound to the bronzes), and wants exactly one thing: the Kleis. Whoever carries the key is its only target. The fun is the running handoff — the key thrown from PC to PC while the building wakes up around them. Killing the Revenant is possible (see stat block) and worth it (its heart is an arcanaite-veined ingot, 250 gp to the right buyer), but staying to try at Heat 4+ should feel greedy.

**Escape routes under pressure:** the cistern (the Revenant won't fit; the hoplites will follow), the skylight rope (the Revenant can't climb; it *can* throw display plinths — +5, 2d10, 60 ft), the front doors (Heat permitting), or — the chaos option — through the Sealed Annex past the Inquisitor while she and the templars turn their flames on the Revenant, because the Truth-Flame hates undead-adjacent bronze more than it hates thieves *tonight*.

### The rival crew

At the DM's chosen moment — ideally the worst one — the crew crosses a [[Tenebral Covenant]] trio led by **Melaina**, mid-burglary in the Founders' Hall, here for the Oath-Prow's gold fittings and whatever's portable. *"No vault is safe when they take interest."* They are professionals: no killing, no noise, no loyalty to anyone in this building. They can become an extra pair of hands (for a cut), a beautiful distraction, or the people who lock a door behind themselves at exactly the wrong time. If the party helped Dexios clear his dice debt, Melaina considers that debt *bought* — and owes the crew one favor, redeemable in the aftermath.

### Delivery

Madame Trioditis waits where she said she would — except she's the **lamplighter** on Museum Hill's lower stair, and has been all night, watching the whole thing (DC 16 Insight at any earlier meeting to have clocked that the widow's shadow fell the wrong way; she is a changeling, like her mistress's chosen). She pays in full, honors the drachmae swap if asked, tells the crew to keep the Lysikleis "as a reference from satisfied clients," and asks one final question as she takes the wrapped key — *"Did it whisper to any of you?"* — and means it.

## The Museum: Wings & Exhibits

*Each wing is a chapter of the city's own story. Fence values are what the Tenebral Covenant would pay; "Heat" is the aftermath cost of the piece going missing.*

### Rotunda of the Concord (ground)

The founding mosaic: settlers of every trade laying down tools together — *The Peaceful Concord*. Four bronze hoplite statues (Mycairan, "decorative") face the doors. Domed skylight above.

### Founders' Hall (ground, west)

The city's beginning — fishermen, miners, traders, scholars who "miraculously chose unity over war."

| Exhibit | What the label says | What's true | Fence / Heat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Oath-Prow** | Figurehead of the first settler ship; the first oaths were sworn on it | True — which is why three noble houses would pay to *suppress* it; their founding oaths are carved on the back in an older alphabet | 400 gp / severe |
| **The Thalos Stele** | "Ceremonial ossuary marker" | A mass-grave stone from the Unity Wars the founding story says never happened | 150 gp; 600 gp to a dissident scholar / moderate |
| First-catch votive nets, mining lamps | Trades of the founders | True | 40 gp / low |

### Hall of the Storm-Star (ground, east)

[[The Continental War]], and the man who ended Hellenoria's part in it: [[Astraphael, the Destructive Star]].

| Exhibit | What the label says | What's true | Fence / Heat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Astraphael's Half-Mantle** | Worn at the unification; storm-scored | The scorch pattern is *from the inside* | 800 gp / extreme — the city riots |
| **Treaty of Myrianth** (illuminated facsimile) | The peace | The facsimile has one clause the public text lacks; the censors missed it (Secrets 8) | 200 gp / high |
| **The Silent Sail** | Sailcloth from the victory fleet | Still damp. Always. Cold to the touch — cut from a ship that never docked | 300 gp to the right cult / high |
| **The Broken Blade** | "A blade shattered in the final duel" | Label recently re-carved; a name has been chiseled out of the plinth — *Sandarion's* | 250 gp / high |

### Gallery of the Violet Dusk (upper)

Why the sky over Aetherion is the color it is — the approved version ("ancient magic").

| Exhibit | What the label says | What's true | Fence / Heat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Aetherglass panes** | Vitrified sand from "the old harbor fire" | Aether-scarred glass from the event the Academy files under *Vault Record 7c*; shows *yesterday's* sky, not today's | 100 gp each (×6) / moderate |
| **The Dusk Prisms** | Academy optical instruments | Point one at the Seal downstairs and the rings' reflection shows a fifth ring that isn't there | 75 gp each (×3) / low |

### The Sealed Annex (upper — restricted, Truth-Flame custody)

Censored history under lock: proscribed manuscripts, Selenarion's original vellum with the Inquisitor's annotations and the dissident's margin-notes, erased records of Iovanius Sandarion. **Inquisitor Callixta Vireon** works here tonight. Nothing here is on the contract; everything here is career-endingly hot (any single manuscript: 500+ gp to the right buyer; Heat: the Truth-Flame opens a permanent file on the crew).

### The Bronze Vault (undercroft — the target)

Everything from the Thalos Ridge dig too dangerous or too true for upstairs: rune tablets repeating **"the gift beneath stone"** (60 gp each ×4 to scholars / moderate), blood-ward bracelets (40 gp ×6 / low), the **Weeping Bronze** colossus head, and the **Kleis Mycarion** under the Seal of the Fourth Mirror.

> **The Kleis Mycarion.** Forearm-long key of layered bronze wound around what is probably bone. Warm. A slow pulse, roughly one beat per breath. Anyone who holds it skin-to-skin dreams of Thalos Ridge that night and must succeed on a DC 12 Wisdom save each dawn or spend the day *knowing which direction the ridge is*. It has no listed fence value because there is exactly one buyer, and she already paid.

### Player prop — the stolen survey

[`the-aletheion-blueprint.svg`](the-aletheion-blueprint.svg) (also as [PNG](the-aletheion-blueprint.png) and [printable PDF](the-aletheion-blueprint.pdf)) — an in-world **survey sheet** of the museum (Office of Works, "the third sheet of seven"), drawn to hand to the players when they start planning. It follows *this module's* floor plan, not Tom Cartos's — it shares his set's three levels (lower, upper, roof access), so the two work side by side, but room-for-room they differ; treat the survey as "old and slightly out of date" if players compare it against whatever battle map you run. It is honest about structure and *dishonest about contents*, exactly as a censored survey would be:

- The Bronze Vault appears only as **"Conservation Store B — see Note 4"**, an empty room; the Seal, the plinth, and the colossus head are nowhere on the sheet. *Where the artifact sits is deliberately not on this map.*
- The Sealed Annex is just **"Archive — Restricted."** No pressure plates, no wards, no ossuary niche.
- The useful truths are in the margins: the oculus is serviced *from the roof*, the cistern grate is "rusted fast," the statues' plinths are "re-set at dawn, daily" — and a dissident hand has scrawled *"re-set daily? why — D."*

**How they get it:** Old Kyros sells the sheet at the Night Agora for 75 gp (it's where the Tenebral Covenant's floor plans come from); Dexios can be talked into "losing" it for an evening; or it's sitting in Curator Eupraxia's office drawer next to the uncensored labels.

## Secrets & Clues

1. Madame Trioditis is a changeling — the widow, the fishmonger, and the lamplighter are one person, and she was watching the museum all night.
2. The Eclipsed Mirror didn't just buy the Lysikleis — they commissioned it from the Virellarion Count *specifically shaped to learn Academy seals*. This job was planned for a year.
3. "Don't touch the key with your skin" is not squeamishness: skin contact starts the Thalos Ridge dreams, and the Mirror needs *carriers*, not converts.
4. Warden Dexios's dice debt is held by the Tenebral Covenant — which is how Melaina's crew got their floor plans.
5. The undercroft is checked only once a night, at midnight — the flooding ruined the lantern brackets and nobody fixed them.
6. The four rotunda hoplites have been changing pose for *years*; the staff rotate them at dawn and log it as "cleaning."
7. The mass graves under Thalos Ridge and the Thalos Stele in Founders' Hall are the same story — the founding was a conquest, and the Kleis was dug from underneath the losers.
8. The treaty facsimile's extra clause cedes "the stone beneath Thalos" to *no signatory* — someone at Myrianth knew the ridge must belong to nobody.
9. Curator Eupraxia keeps Selenarion's uncensored labels in her desk; if the crew ever needs leverage against the Truth-Flame, it's a drawer away.
10. In every mirror-ring of the Seal, the reflection is one instant *behind* — except the innermost ring, which is one instant *ahead*.

## Aftermath & Sequel Hooks

- **The job clean:** 1,500 gp, the Lysikleis, and a satisfied occult society that now considers the crew *proven*. Next contract: escort work to Thalos Ridge. They'll even be honest about it. Mostly.
- **The key delivered:** within a season, the ridge. *The gift beneath stone* is a campaign, not an epilogue — and the bronzes of the city weep the day the door opens.
- **The key kept or diverted:** whoever holds it dreams of the ridge; the Mirror does not send angry letters, it sends the [[Crimson Pact]]. Phalaina's 3,000 gp offer still stands. The Truth-Flame's file grows either way.
- **The Revenant destroyed, quietly:** the Academy quietly pays a "finder's honorarium" (200 gp) through the museum, no questions asked in a way that is itself a question.
- **Melaina's favor:** one door in Hellenoria, any city, unlocked. She means it literally.
